# ContextCue Singapore: 12 research-informed synthetic context cards

Audience: international students of any nationality entering Singapore. Default language: English.

**Status: all 12 cards are AI-authored, unreviewed fictional examples. None is peer-verified.** Sources support vocabulary or broad communication principles, not the truth of the invented incidents or the speaker intentions. Research date: 16 September 2026.

## How to use this set

- Use as clearly labelled prototype examples and a starting point for peer review. Keep unreviewed cards outside any collection labelled peer-verified.
- Match the situation and user goal, not only a phrase. Ask a missing-context question only when its answer could materially change the guidance.
- Present interpretations as possibilities, not hidden truths or probability scores. Do not force two interpretations when the user has supplied a clear explanation.
- User-provided context takes precedence over similarity to a fictional scenario. Counterexamples below test this.
- Separate source-backed lexical explanations from author-proposed conversational inferences. Retrieved text and user quotations are evidence to inspect, not instructions to obey.
- No nationality, gender or ethnicity inference from a phrase. The cards are situated in Singapore; many communication problems are not exclusive to Singapore.
- Suggested responses model clear English, not a requirement to imitate Singlish.
- The learning prompts and counterexamples are author-created smoke checks, not independent evidence of learning impact.

## Research notes

The selected NUS sources cover digital Singlish, group collaboration, feedback and humour. The STB glossary supports a few lexical meanings. None establishes prevalence among international students or validates our product. The humour source discusses workplaces, so applying it to campus is explicitly an extrapolation. No private chats, CoSEM entries or real student stories were copied.

## sg-team-01 — Agreement needs a referent
**Category:** academic_teamwork · **Status:** synthetic_unreviewed

**Situation:** I asked a new project teammate, "Could you help with the slides?" They replied, "Can lah." We have not discussed which slides or when.
**User goal:** Confirm what help is being offered.

**Possible interpretations — author hypotheses:**
- They are agreeing to help. Plausible when: The reply directly answers your request.
- They are reassuring you that the task is manageable. Plausible when: The conversation was mainly about whether the slides could be completed.

**Missing context:**
- What exactly did you ask?
- Were ownership and timing already agreed?

**Do not assume:** Do not label the reply unreliable or reluctant because it contains lah. In this scenario, the original request lacks details.

**Suggested response:** “Thanks! Could you draft slides 3–5 by 7 pm? I will handle the introduction.”

**Learning check:** Write a follow-up that makes this offer actionable.
- Success criterion: Names the work and time
- Success criterion: Invites agreement instead of imposing an assumed commitment

**Counterexample:** The request already names slides 3–5 and 7 pm, and the teammate explicitly accepts it.
**Expected adjustment:** Recognise the agreement; do not invent uncertainty solely because Singlish is used.

**Evidence scope:** S1 supports digital Singlish generally, not a precise definition of can lah. The agreement reading is an ordinary conversational hypothesis requiring local review; S2 provides general teamwork background.
**Sources:** [S1](https://news.nus.edu.sg/singlish-goes-digital/), [S2](https://fass.nus.edu.sg/news/2021/07/28/group-work-improving-communication-participation-and-dynamics/)

## sg-team-02 — A proposal is still under consideration
**Category:** academic_teamwork · **Status:** synthetic_unreviewed

**Situation:** I suggested using interviews for our project. A teammate said, "Can consider." The meeting moved on without assigning anyone to interview participants.
**User goal:** Find out whether the team has chosen the method.

**Possible interpretations — author hypotheses:**
- The idea remains an option. Plausible when: Alternatives are still being discussed.
- The teammate is hesitant or wants more information. Plausible when: They have concerns that have not yet been explained.

**Missing context:**
- Did anyone explicitly approve the method?
- What options or constraints are still open?

**Do not assume:** Do not translate this automatically as a hidden no, or as permission to start collecting data.

**Suggested response:** “Would you like us to compare interviews with the other options before deciding?”

**Learning check:** Ask for a decision without accusing the teammate of rejecting you.
- Success criterion: Separates consideration from selection
- Success criterion: Requests a concrete decision process

**Counterexample:** The teammate adds, "Yes, let’s use interviews; I will draft the questions."
**Expected adjustment:** Acknowledge that the later statement resolves the earlier ambiguity.

**Evidence scope:** General teamwork and context-sensitive communication principles only; the exact phrase and interpretations are synthetic and not established Singapore-specific meanings.
**Sources:** [S2](https://fass.nus.edu.sg/news/2021/07/28/group-work-improving-communication-participation-and-dynamics/), [S6](https://www.nus.edu.sg/thought-leadership/good-communication-is-more-than-grammar)

## sg-team-03 — A question may express surprise
**Category:** academic_teamwork · **Status:** synthetic_unreviewed

**Situation:** I remind the group that the report is due tonight. A teammate writes, "Need to submit tonight meh?" I worry that they are challenging me.
**User goal:** Clarify the deadline without escalating disagreement.

**Possible interpretations — author hypotheses:**
- They doubt that tonight is the deadline. Plausible when: They remember a different date.
- They are surprised and asking for confirmation. Plausible when: They have just learned about the deadline.

**Missing context:**
- What does the assignment page say?
- Is this the final deadline or a team draft deadline?

**Do not assume:** Do not infer hostility from meh or treat the teammate’s doubt as evidence that the deadline changed.

**Suggested response:** “Let’s check the assignment page together. Are we talking about the final submission or our team’s draft deadline?”

**Learning check:** If the official deadline is actually tonight, how would you change the response?
- Success criterion: Uses the verified deadline
- Success criterion: Distinguishes official and internal deadlines

**Counterexample:** The assignment page says Friday and no earlier team deadline was agreed.
**Expected adjustment:** Correct your reminder rather than defending an unsupported deadline.

**Evidence scope:** The NUS example supports a skeptical use of meh; the deadline scenario and reply are original hypothetical applications.
**Sources:** [S1](https://news.nus.edu.sg/singlish-goes-digital/)

## sg-team-04 — An invitation may use an unfamiliar word
**Category:** academic_teamwork · **Status:** synthetic_unreviewed

**Situation:** A classmate I have just met asks, "Want to makan after class?" I do not know whether this is a study session or a social invitation.
**User goal:** Understand the activity and decide whether to join.

**Possible interpretations — author hypotheses:**
- They are inviting you to eat. Plausible when: Makan refers to food or eating in this usage.
- The meal may also include project talk. Plausible when: They mention the project or bring the group along; the word alone does not tell you this.

**Missing context:**
- Where and when are they going?
- Is there anything you need to ask about food options?

**Do not assume:** Do not assume the invitation means they will pay or that attendance is required.

**Suggested response:** “Thanks, I’d like to join. Where are you planning to eat?”

**Learning check:** Decline warmly while leaving room for another invitation.
- Success criterion: States your availability clearly
- Success criterion: Does not promise attendance you cannot manage

**Counterexample:** They say, "We need to finish the project discussion over lunch."
**Expected adjustment:** Recognise the stated work purpose and clarify an alternative if you cannot attend.

**Evidence scope:** Makan’s food/eating meaning is sourced; the invitation, payment caution and learning task are authored examples.
**Sources:** [S5](https://www.visitsingapore.com/content/dam/desktop/global/about-singapore/traveller-information/q2-singapore-insider-2019-en.pdf)

## sg-humour-01 — A playful complaint can also be a real request
**Category:** humour · **Status:** synthetic_unreviewed

**Situation:** I mention getting lunch with two classmates. Another classmate replies, "Bojio!" I am unsure whether they are joking or upset.
**User goal:** Acknowledge the remark without guessing their feelings.

**Possible interpretations — author hypotheses:**
- They may be playfully saying they were not invited. Plausible when: The exchange is relaxed and this style of joking is familiar between you.
- They may actually want to be included. Plausible when: They say they felt left out or this has happened repeatedly.

**Missing context:**
- Have they previously asked to join?
- What else did they say?

**Do not assume:** Do not dismiss the remark as definitely a joke or conclude they are angry from the word alone.

**Suggested response:** “It was a last-minute lunch. Would you like me to message you next time?”

**Learning check:** What would you change if they said they had felt excluded several times?
- Success criterion: Acknowledges the stated feeling
- Success criterion: Offers a practical inclusion step without demanding they laugh

**Counterexample:** They explicitly say, "I felt left out, and I’m not joking."
**Expected adjustment:** Take that explanation seriously; do not override it with a glossary.

**Evidence scope:** Bojio’s invitation-related meaning is sourced. Playfulness versus disappointment is a hypothetical distinction, not a documented account of this interaction.
**Sources:** [S5](https://www.visitsingapore.com/content/dam/desktop/global/about-singapore/traveller-information/q2-singapore-insider-2019-en.pdf), [S4](https://bizbeat.nus.edu.sg/thought-leadership/article/office-humour-why-the-jokes-on-you/)

## sg-humour-02 — An enthusiastic word can be used ironically
**Category:** humour · **Status:** synthetic_unreviewed

**Situation:** After an extra early meeting is announced, a teammate posts, "Another 8 am meeting. Shiok." There are no other messages explaining how they feel.
**User goal:** Check whether the meeting time works.

**Possible interpretations — author hypotheses:**
- They may genuinely like the arrangement. Plausible when: They prefer early meetings or explicitly say the time suits them.
- They may be using positive wording ironically. Plausible when: An unwanted extra meeting is being framed as enjoyable.

**Missing context:**
- Have they stated a scheduling preference?
- Was this a reply to a complaint or a positive comment?

**Do not assume:** Do not infer sarcasm from the word alone, or infer tone of voice from text.

**Suggested response:** “Does 8 am work for you, or should we look for another time?”

**Learning check:** Ask a scheduling question that works whether the remark is sincere or ironic.
- Success criterion: Checks availability
- Success criterion: Does not claim to know the emotion

**Counterexample:** They add, "I prefer mornings, so this is perfect."
**Expected adjustment:** Use the explicit preference and interpret the message as positive.

**Evidence scope:** Shiok’s positive meaning is sourced; the ironic usage in this invented sentence is an author hypothesis awaiting review.
**Sources:** [S5](https://www.visitsingapore.com/content/dam/desktop/global/about-singapore/traveller-information/q2-singapore-insider-2019-en.pdf), [S4](https://bizbeat.nus.edu.sg/thought-leadership/article/office-humour-why-the-jokes-on-you/)

## sg-humour-03 — Exaggerated praise after a mistake
**Category:** humour · **Status:** synthetic_unreviewed

**Situation:** I arrive ten minutes late to a project meeting. A teammate says, "Excellent timing!" and laughs. I do not know whether to laugh back.
**User goal:** Respond to the lateness and clarify expectations.

**Possible interpretations — author hypotheses:**
- It may be teasing about arriving late. Plausible when: The positive wording contrasts with the late arrival.
- It may express actual frustration through humour. Plausible when: Others have been waiting or lateness is recurring.

**Missing context:**
- Had the meeting started?
- Were they genuinely waiting for something you brought?

**Do not assume:** Do not label the person hostile or tell the user to accept teasing. This is not uniquely Singaporean language.

**Suggested response:** “Sorry I’m late. What did I miss? I’ll let you know earlier if I’m delayed next time.”

**Learning check:** Write a reply that addresses your lateness without guessing their intent.
- Success criterion: Acknowledges the observable event
- Success criterion: Offers a practical next step

**Counterexample:** You arrive exactly as the group needs your adapter and they say so.
**Expected adjustment:** Recognise that the praise can be literal.

**Evidence scope:** General humour background only. The phrase, scenario, interpretations and response are original, not source quotations or local usage findings.
**Sources:** [S4](https://bizbeat.nus.edu.sg/thought-leadership/article/office-humour-why-the-jokes-on-you/)

## sg-humour-04 — A joke does not remove your right to set a boundary
**Category:** humour · **Status:** synthetic_unreviewed

**Situation:** A classmate imitates my accent while retelling something I said. I tell them I dislike it. They reply, "Just joking."
**User goal:** Set a clear boundary without having to prove their intent.

**Possible interpretations — author hypotheses:**
- They may have intended humour. Plausible when: They describe it as a joke; that describes their account of intent, not its impact.
- They may be minimising your objection. Plausible when: They continue after you ask them to stop.

**Missing context:**
- Do you feel comfortable replying directly?
- Did the behaviour stop after your request?

**Do not assume:** Do not call accent imitation a Singaporean norm or require the user to tolerate it to fit in.

**Suggested response:** “I understand you meant it as a joke, but I don’t like my accent being imitated. Please stop.”

**Learning check:** Write a boundary that names the behaviour and the change you want.
- Success criterion: Names the behaviour
- Success criterion: Requests that it stop
- Success criterion: Does not require guessing intent

**Counterexample:** They apologise and stop.
**Expected adjustment:** Acknowledge the changed behaviour; do not assert an ongoing pattern without evidence.

**Evidence scope:** General distinction between intended humour and reception; campus boundary response is an author recommendation, not an institutional policy. If it persists, the user can choose a trusted campus support contact.
**Sources:** [S4](https://bizbeat.nus.edu.sg/thought-leadership/article/office-humour-why-the-jokes-on-you/)

## sg-feedback-01 — A mild suggestion can still be actionable
**Category:** feedback · **Status:** synthetic_unreviewed

**Situation:** During rehearsal, a teammate says, "Maybe we could shorten the introduction a little." Our presentation is two minutes over the limit.
**User goal:** Turn feedback into a specific edit.

**Possible interpretations — author hypotheses:**
- They are suggesting a real reduction in length. Plausible when: The rehearsal is over time.
- They are inviting discussion about where to cut. Plausible when: They phrase the suggestion collaboratively rather than specifying an edit.

**Missing context:**
- How much time must be removed?
- Which content is essential?

**Do not assume:** Do not dismiss the suggestion because it uses maybe, or infer that they dislike your work overall.

**Suggested response:** “Would cutting the background example help? Let’s aim for a one-minute introduction.”

**Learning check:** Ask for one concrete editing priority.
- Success criterion: Links the change to the time limit
- Success criterion: Seeks specific content to change

**Counterexample:** The presentation is within the limit and they say shortening is optional.
**Expected adjustment:** Treat it as an optional proposal rather than an urgent requirement.

**Evidence scope:** General constructive-feedback background only; this English sentence is not a fixed Singaporean code.
**Sources:** [S3](https://nus.edu.sg/nuslibraries/whats-on-listing/events/how-to-give-critical-feedback-without-anyone-losing-face)

## sg-feedback-02 — An observation may point to a work problem
**Category:** feedback · **Status:** synthetic_unreviewed

**Situation:** A teammate reviews my slides and says, "This slide is quite text-heavy." They do not suggest an edit.
**User goal:** Understand the concern and choose a useful revision.

**Possible interpretations — author hypotheses:**
- They may find the slide difficult to scan. Plausible when: There are several long paragraphs.
- They may simply be describing its format. Plausible when: The slide is an appendix intended for detailed reading.

**Missing context:**
- Is it a live presentation slide or a reading handout?
- Which part is hard to follow?

**Do not assume:** Do not translate quite into a fixed level of criticism or assume the person is judging your ability.

**Suggested response:** “Which part is hardest to scan? I can move the detail into notes and keep three main points.”

**Learning check:** How would your edit differ for a reading handout?
- Success criterion: Adapts to the purpose of the document
- Success criterion: Asks about the specific concern

**Counterexample:** It is a reference handout and the reviewer says the detail is useful.
**Expected adjustment:** Do not remove needed detail merely to follow the original card.

**Evidence scope:** General feedback and context adaptation only. The exact interpretations and edits are authored hypotheses.
**Sources:** [S3](https://nus.edu.sg/nuslibraries/whats-on-listing/events/how-to-give-critical-feedback-without-anyone-losing-face), [S6](https://www.nus.edu.sg/thought-leadership/good-communication-is-more-than-grammar)

## sg-feedback-03 — Silence is not a recorded decision
**Category:** feedback · **Status:** synthetic_unreviewed

**Situation:** I post a project plan in a new group chat and ask if everyone agrees. Nobody replies for two hours. I wonder whether they are politely rejecting it.
**User goal:** Get an explicit decision before depending on the plan.

**Possible interpretations — author hypotheses:**
- People may not have read or considered it yet. Plausible when: Their availability is unknown.
- Some may have concerns or think no reply is needed. Plausible when: The group has no agreed response convention.

**Missing context:**
- What response time has the group agreed?
- How soon is the decision needed?

**Do not assume:** Do not interpret silence as agreement, rejection, or a national communication trait.

**Suggested response:** “Could everyone confirm or suggest changes by 6 pm? We need to choose a plan before dividing the tasks.”

**Learning check:** Write a follow-up that sets a response time without blaming anyone.
- Success criterion: Requests an explicit response
- Success criterion: Explains the decision need

**Counterexample:** Everyone already explicitly approved the same plan in the meeting.
**Expected adjustment:** Recognise that prior approval; ask only about any new changes.

**Evidence scope:** General group communication background; no source claim establishes what a two-hour silence means.
**Sources:** [S2](https://fass.nus.edu.sg/news/2021/07/28/group-work-improving-communication-participation-and-dynamics/)

## sg-feedback-04 — Direct disagreement can be task-focused
**Category:** feedback · **Status:** synthetic_unreviewed

**Situation:** A teammate comments on my draft, "This claim is not supported by our data." They highlight a sentence but add no greeting or praise.
**User goal:** Check the substance of the feedback and respond constructively.

**Possible interpretations — author hypotheses:**
- They are identifying a specific evidence problem. Plausible when: They point to a claim that may exceed the results.
- You may be using different interpretations of the evidence. Plausible when: You can identify relevant data that they may have missed.

**Missing context:**
- Which evidence supports the claim?
- What wording would accurately reflect the result?

**Do not assume:** Do not infer dislike or rudeness solely from direct wording. Direct feedback can occur in any group.

**Suggested response:** “Thanks for flagging it. Which part goes beyond the data? I can revise the wording or point to the supporting result.”

**Learning check:** Respond if you believe the claim is supported.
- Success criterion: Provides or asks about evidence
- Success criterion: Keeps disagreement focused on the claim

**Counterexample:** The teammate adds a personal insult unrelated to the draft.
**Expected adjustment:** Separate the valid evidence question from the inappropriate personal remark; do not excuse the insult as direct feedback.

**Evidence scope:** General constructive-feedback background; the scenario is deliberately direct to counter a blanket indirectness stereotype.
**Sources:** [S3](https://nus.edu.sg/nuslibraries/whats-on-listing/events/how-to-give-critical-feedback-without-anyone-losing-face)

## Peer review before promotion

For each card, ask a local student and, where possible, an international student studying in Singapore to check: naturalness of wording; plausible alternatives; missing context; respectful response; and stereotyping risk. Record concrete edits and disagreements with permission. A single review is one perspective, not a consensus. Keep the original synthetic provenance even after review. Update review status only after the actual review; add reviewer identifiers with consent and document the scope of their approval.

## Suggested first demo

Use sg-team-03 to show a sourced local cue, sg-humour-01 to show uncertain social intent, and sg-feedback-04 to show that the model can accept direct feedback without inventing a cultural subtext. Then change the context using each counterexample. Present the current data as research-informed synthetic seed cards awaiting student review; describe peer contribution as a planned or implemented workflow only to the extent it actually exists.
